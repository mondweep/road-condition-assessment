Your phone records motion and location only — no camera, no microphone. The first 300 m of every trip and roughly the last 400 m are never uploaded, so your start and end points stay private. The public map only shows road stretches, never individual trips. Uploads carry a random device ID and the vehicle type you enter; no name or phone number.

🛣️

Map bad roads just by driving

Your phone feels how the vehicle moves and turns that into a road-condition map anyone can see.

  • Shaking shows how rough the road is
  • Sharp jolts suggest potholes you hit
  • Swerves mark potholes drivers avoid
  • Crawling and lurching shows near-impassable stretches

It uses vibration and GPS only, so it works at night and in dust, with no camera.

📱

Mount the phone firmly

  • Use a dashboard or windscreen holder.
  • Not in a pocket, cupholder, on the seat or held in your hand. A loose phone makes every road look terrible.
  • Any angle is fine. The app works out which way is up.
  • Plug in a charger. The screen has to stay on.
▶️

Press Start, then just drive

  • Press Start recording before setting off.
  • Don't touch the phone while moving. Leave this screen open: if it locks or you switch apps, recording pauses.
  • Drive normally, including slowly on bad roads. Crawling counts too.
  • When you arrive, stop and press Stop. Readings upload whenever there's signal, so dead zones are fine.
🗺️

Reading the map

Good   Fair   Poor   Very poor

  • A black ring means a sharp jolt was felt there, often a pothole (sometimes a speed breaker).
  • A ⚠ triangle means drivers on several trips swerved there. Something is probably being avoided.
  • One trip is a hint. Several trips on the same road make it reliable, so drive your regular routes often.

Privacy: the start and end of every trip are never uploaded.
