How this map works

Drivers mount a phone in their vehicle and run the recorder. It uses the phone's motion sensors and GPS. There's no camera, so it works at night and in dust. Each dot is a ~40 m stretch of road.

What's measured

  • Roughness: how much the vehicle shakes up and down, adjusted for speed, since faster driving shakes more on the same road.
  • Sharp jolts (black ring): a single hard bump against a calm background. Usually a pothole, sometimes a speed breaker or rail crossing.
  • Swerves (⚠): a quick steer out and back on a straight road. One swerve could be a cow or an overtake. ⚠ appears only when drivers on several separate trips swerve at the same spot.
  • Crawling: on very bad roads drivers slow to walking pace. The app counts the lurches as each wheel drops instead of measuring shaking. Crawling with no lurches is ignored, because it's probably traffic.

How accurate is it?

  • One trip is a hint; several trips on the same road are reliable. Click a dot to see how many trips it's based on.
  • Potholes that every driver steers around are only detectable through swerves.
  • Different vehicles feel the same road differently. An SUV smooths out bumps a hatchback feels.
  • Positions are GPS-accurate, typically to within 5–15 m.
  • UK and North East India are scored against different standards. "Poor" in Kent is a smaller problem than "poor" on a rural Assam road.
  • The thresholds are early estimates and are being tuned against labelled drives.

Privacy

The first 300 m and roughly the last 400 m of every trip are never uploaded. This map shows only road stretches, never individual journeys.
